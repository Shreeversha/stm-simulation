Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM) — Tunneling Current vs. Distance

An STM works by quantum tunneling: electrons can cross the tiny vacuum gap between a sharp metal tip and a conducting surface even though classically they lack the energy to do so. The resulting tunneling current decays exponentially with tip–sample distance, I ∝ e−2κd, so a change of just one Ångström changes the current by roughly an order of magnitude. In real instruments this extreme sensitivity is what lets the STM resolve individual atoms.
